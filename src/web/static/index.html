Project 5: Retail Customer Segmentation (CO3 | L4)

Machine Learning - II | Behavioral Segmentation via Unsupervised Clustering
Course Outcome: CO3 (Unsupervised Learning) Bloom's Taxonomy: L4 (Analyze & Evaluate) Official UCI Online Retail (541K Records)
Executive Problem Statement: Retail enterprises generate millions of transaction logs, but raw transactional data is inert without behavioral aggregation. This project engineers multi-dimensional customer features (RFM, basket breadth, regularity, tenure), evaluates multiple clustering paradigms (K-Means, GMM, Agglomerative, DBSCAN, SOM), validates topological consistency via Adjusted Rand Index, and transforms mathematical centroids into actionable marketing strategies and quantified Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

1. Data Collection & Preprocessing Rigor

Unlike pre-aggregated datasets (e.g. Mall Customers), the UCI Online Retail dataset provides raw invoice-level transactions. True data cleaning requires domain-justified decisions rather than blanket dropna():

  • Cancellations: Invoices starting with 'C' are dropped to reflect realized purchases and prevent negative-quantity artifacts.
  • Non-Product Items: Administrative codes (POSTAGE, BANK CHARGES, ADJUST) stripped to preserve basket semantics.
  • Missing Identity: 131,382 guest checkout transactions without Customer IDs safely pruned (imputation of identity is statistically invalid).
  • Outlier Winsorization: Capped bulk wholesale anomalies at 99.5th percentile to protect Euclidean distance calculations without losing real customer entities.

2. Feature Engineering & Mathematical Transformation

Features are computed per customer: Recency (days relative to snapshot), Frequency (distinct invoices), Monetary Total (sum of line totals), Average Order Value, Average Basket Size, Category Diversity, and Purchase Regularity. Because monetary and frequency distributions exhibit heavy right-skewness, we apply a $\log(1 + x)$ transformation followed by Z-score Standardization:

z = \frac{\log(1 + x) - \mu}{\sigma}

3. Algorithmic Techniques & Cross-Validation

To satisfy CO3 Module 3 and Module 4 learning objectives, multiple clustering paradigms were deployed and compared:

  • K-Means Clustering: Centroid-based partitioning minimizing within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS). Optimal K evaluated across Elbow curve and Silhouette score.
  • Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM): Expectation-Maximization probabilistic clustering accommodating ellipsoidal variance and soft assignment.
  • Hierarchical / Agglomerative Clustering: Ward's linkage clustering constructing a variance-minimizing merge tree.
  • DBSCAN: Density-based spatial clustering isolating arbitrary-shaped clusters and identifying 71 outlier customer anomalies.
  • Self-Organizing Maps (SOM): Unsupervised competitive neural network preserving non-linear topological neighborhoods on a 2D lattice.
  • Adjusted Rand Index (ARI): Measures agreement between K-Means and GMM (achieved 0.747), verifying that clusters reflect genuine consumer behaviors rather than algorithm artifacts.

4. Business Performance Impact & Targeted Marketing

Traditional mass marketing allocates budget uniformly across customer lists, producing high churn and poor marketing ROI. Segmented marketing enables targeted capital allocation:

  • Champions: Require loyalty advocacy, early product drops, and concierge care (discount sensitivity is very low; price slashing erodes brand equity).
  • At-Risk High-Value: Represent the highest return-on-marketing investment. Even a 18% win-back conversion preserves over £29,000 in gross margin that would otherwise be permanently lost.
  • Hibernating: Approached via zero-marginal-cost automated email clearance blasts; suppressed from expensive direct channels.
